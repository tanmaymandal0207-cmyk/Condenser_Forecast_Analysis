# Swift Condenser Demand Gap — Client Report

**Client:** automotive parts distributor (anonymised) · **Status:** draft for validation · **Data:** challan dispatch history Apr 2009 – May 2019

---

## 1. Executive summary

Dispatches of the client's Swift / Swift Dzire AC condenser, **PC-86207**, peaked at **~580 units in FY 2012–13** and fell to **~40 units in FY 2014–15**.

The decline is real in the client's own records, but it does not yet tell us *why*. Three explanations are still open:

1. Demand for the vehicle fitment itself fell.
2. Demand moved to a different product code (PC-86539 is the leading candidate).
3. Distributors moved some purchasing to other suppliers or channels.

This report sets out what the data shows today, what it cannot show yet, and the specific checks needed before a commercial decision is made.

## 2. Business question

> Did demand for the underlying vehicle fitment decline, or did demand shift to alternative product codes, suppliers or distribution channels?

## 3. What the data shows

| Area | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| SKU trajectory | ~580 units (FY 2012–13) → ~40 units (FY 2014–15) | High: from challan totals |
| Code transition | PC-86539 appears in descriptions for Swift, Ertiga and SX4 | Medium: its volume is not Swift-only |
| Distributor behaviour | Purchasing concentration is examined across distributors | Low: one SKU's drop does not prove a supplier switch |
| Vehicle base | Swift / Dzire cohorts are relevant to AC replacement demand | Indicator only: not a replacement volume |
| Pricing | Indicative comparison against selected competitors | Low: quotes are not like-for-like or dated |

## 4. What the data cannot show yet

- Swift-specific volume inside multi-fitment codes such as PC-86539.
- Whether post-2019 figures hold up. *Update 18 Nov 2026:* the 2019–2026 yearly files are now loaded and reconciled to their own totals ([status report](migration-status-report.md)). Code-level figures stay provisional until the client confirms 170 new codes and the Apr–May 2019 overlap.
- Whether any distributor replaced the client with another supplier.
- Current, comparable market prices.

## 5. Recommended validation steps

| # | Step | Data needed | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Classify PC-86539 lines by vehicle application | Line-level descriptions, OEM references | How much of the "successor" volume is Swift |
| 2 | Reconcile post-2019 code-level figures | Original transaction records | Whether recent demand recovered |
| 3 | Distributor purchase history across all condenser codes | Full purchase ledger by distributor | Whether demand moved code, or moved supplier |
| 4 | Dated, like-for-like competitor quotes | Spec, fitment, quality, taxes, margins | Whether price explains lost share |
| 5 | Vehicle cohort sizing for the repair age | Registration / sales cohorts | Size of the addressable replacement market |

## 5a. Update from the 2019–2026 data

- PC-86207 still sells in small volumes: 157 units in FY 2019–20, falling to 14 in Apr–Oct 2026.
- The client's master list names **PC-86539** as *Swift (new)* (14,135 units Apr 2019 – Oct 2026 with its Q5 grade) and a separate **PC-86815** for the *Swift 2017* generation (1,606 units since Jun 2019).
- This supports a code transition by vehicle generation. Step 1 is still needed, because challan descriptions for PC-86539 also name other models.

## 6. Decisions this supports

- Whether to keep, re-launch or retire PC-86207.
- Which Swift / Dzire fitments to prioritise for market validation.
- Which distributors to review for share loss.
- Where a product-code gap exists in the current range.

## 7. Limitations

No single cause is claimed for the decline. Code succession and supplier substitution are hypotheses until confirmed with transaction-level evidence. Vehicle population is a demand indicator, not a confirmed sales volume.
